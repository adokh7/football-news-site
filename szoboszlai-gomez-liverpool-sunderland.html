Szoboszlai's Nashville Screamer Saves Iraola's Liverpool Debut — Then Gomez Delivers the Real Blow

Liverpool won 4–2. Dominik Szoboszlai supplied the picture-perfect moment. Yet Joe Gomez limping out inside 10 minutes was the sight that should keep Andoni Iraola awake.

By Govolsgo Staff 26 July 2026 6 min read
Share X f

Match at a glance

  • Liverpool beat Sunderland 4–2 at GEODIS Park, Nashville, in front of 24,897.
  • Szoboszlai levelled with a chest control and half-volley from just outside the box.
  • Joe Gomez suffered an apparent muscle injury inside the opening 10 minutes.

Fifty-six minutes. One loose clearance. One touch on the chest. Then violence.

Dominik Szoboszlai met the bouncing ball just outside Sunderland's box and buried a half-volley inside Melker Ellborg's near post before the goalkeeper had even set himself. That made it 2–2. Liverpool went on to beat Sunderland 4–2 at GEODIS Park in Nashville, watched by 24,897.

Call it a screamer. Call it a rescue act. Just don't call it a 25-yard volley. It wasn't. The ball was controlled on Szoboszlai's chest, then struck on the bounce from outside the area with a defender bearing down. The accurate description does not shrink the goal. It explains why it was so good.

Four goals, three swings, one ruthless bench

Liverpool had started sharply. On 12 minutes, Harvey Elliott slipped Kieran Morrison down the right. Morrison cut inside and rolled a low finish into the corner. Clean. Direct. One-nil.

Sunderland bit back on 28 minutes when Enzo Le Fée drove home from range beyond Giorgi Mamardashvili. Four minutes after half-time, Tom Proctor won the aerial duel and Timur Tutierov converted the knock-down from close range. Liverpool had gone from control to chasing the game.

Then Iraola emptied his hand. Szoboszlai replaced Elliott at the interval, joined by Trey Nyoni, Milos Kerkez, Federico Chiesa and Lewis Koumas. Sunderland also changed their side heavily at half-time, and the match tilted hard.

Liverpool took 73 per cent of the second-half possession, won the shot count 13–6 and completed 261 passes to Sunderland's 76. Szoboszlai equalised. Federico Chiesa put Liverpool ahead from the corner of the six-yard box after Calvin Ramsay's pass. With four minutes left, Szoboszlai fed Lewis Koumas, who stepped inside Leo Hjelde and finished from 10 yards.

Szoboszlai looks built for the rebuild

This was only a friendly in the thick Tennessee humidity. Nobody sensible is planning a parade. Still, Liverpool needed signs of life after a season that finished with them fifth, 25 points behind champions Arsenal, and ended with Arne Slot being sacked.

Szoboszlai supplied one. Again. He scored 13 goals and made 12 more in 53 appearances last season, numbers that earned him Liverpool's player-of-the-year vote. On 17 July he signed a new five-year extension through to 2031. He is not merely surviving this rebuild. He is starting to look like its spine.

There is a leadership opening, too. Andy Robertson left as a free agent and joined Tottenham, leaving Liverpool in need of a vice-captain. Szoboszlai's case is becoming difficult to ignore.

Gomez is the headline Iraola cannot dodge

And yet the best goal of the night was not the biggest story. Joe Gomez pulled up before 10 minutes had passed. The problem appeared muscular. He was replaced by 18-year-old Ifeanyi Ndukwe and travelled back to Chicago for tests.

Iraola knew what mattered. In his first post-match interview as Liverpool manager, he did not mention Szoboszlai once. He spoke about heavy legs and humidity, then called Gomez's injury the worst news of the evening.

Look at the centre-back options and the concern makes perfect sense. Ibrahima Konaté left for nothing at the end of last season. Giovanni Leoni remains in ACL recovery. Jeremy Jacquet missed this match with a knock. That leaves 35-year-old Virgil van Dijk standing almost alone, with hope doing far too much of the squad planning.

New faces waiting in Chicago

Alexander Isak, Florian Wirtz and Ryan Gravenberch were already waiting back in Chicago after their World Cup break. Iraola confirmed the trio would begin training there. Their arrival adds glamour. It does not patch the hole Gomez may have left.

Next comes Wrexham at Yankee Stadium on 29 July. Liverpool then close the tour against Leeds on 2 August. Iraola has his first win, Szoboszlai has his first thunderbolt of the new era, and the attack already looks lively.

But if the scans bring bad news, the final score in Nashville will become background noise. Liverpool won the match. Their defence may have lost its last margin for error.
